South Australia's rental laws have changed in six main steps since April 2023: smaller bonds, a ban on rent bidding, one rent increase a year, the big July 2024 reforms (reasons needed to end a tenancy, longer notice, inspection limits, pets and minimum standards), a new ground to end a tenancy when you're selling in September 2025, and a compulsory application form from January 2026. Here's what each one means for landlords, as at October 2026.

The official summary is on the Consumer and Business Services (CBS) [rental reforms page](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/campaigns/rental-reforms). The law itself is the [Residential Tenancies Act 1995](https://www.legislation.sa.gov.au/lz?path=%2Fc%2Fa%2Fresidential%20tenancies%20act%201995).

## 1. Bonds: 4 weeks for most rentals (1 April 2023)

**What changed:** Since 1 April 2023, the maximum bond is 4 weeks' rent where the weekly rent is $800 or less. Above $800 a week, you can ask for up to 6 weeks. The old cut-off was $250 a week ([Law Handbook: Bonds](https://lawhandbook.sa.gov.au/ch23s01s05s01.php)).

**What to do:** Check the bond on every new lease against the $800 threshold. CBS says the same limits apply to any bond top-ups on bonds set up before 1 April 2023 ([CBS summary](https://cbs.sa.gov.au/documents/tenancy/fact-sheets/Summary-of-tenancy-reforms-landlords-and-agents.pdf)).

Since 1 July 2024, tenants can also lodge their own bond with the Commissioner for Consumer Affairs ([Law Handbook: Bonds](https://lawhandbook.sa.gov.au/ch23s01s05s01.php)). See our guide to [rental bonds in SA](/blog/rental-bonds-sa-landlords/).

## 2. Rent bidding banned (1 September 2023)

**What changed:** CBS says soliciting rent bidding has been banned since 1 September 2023. A rental must be advertised at a fixed amount, and you can't invite offers above it.

**What to do:** No rent ranges, no "offers over" and no rent auctions. Anyone handling applications for you also can't rate an applicant higher because they've offered more rent ([CBS rental reforms](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/campaigns/rental-reforms)).

## 3. One rent increase a year and tougher penalties (1 March 2024)

**What changed:** Rent can't be increased more than once in 12 months, including when a fixed-term lease rolls over to periodic, and even if the tenant agrees. At the same time, penalties across the Act went up, and a landlord can't unreasonably refuse a tenant permission to sub-let, or charge a fee for agreeing ([CBS rental reforms](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/campaigns/rental-reforms), [CBS summary](https://cbs.sa.gov.au/documents/tenancy/fact-sheets/Summary-of-tenancy-reforms-landlords-and-agents.pdf)).

**What to do:** Keep a record of the date the lease started and the date of each increase, and plan reviews around them. A rent increase needs at least 60 days' written notice, and a tenant has 90 days to ask SACAT whether it's excessive ([Law Handbook: Rent](https://lawhandbook.sa.gov.au/ch23s01s06s05.php)). Our guide to [rent increases in SA](/blog/rent-increases-sa-landlords/) goes through it step by step.

## 4. The major reforms (1 July 2024)

CBS calls these the state's biggest tenancy reforms in 30 years, and they affect nearly every part of managing a rental.

### Ending a tenancy needs a prescribed reason

You can no longer end, or decline to renew, a tenancy without one of the reasons set out in the regulations ([CBS: reforms for landlords and agents](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/sections/renting/renting/tenancy-reforms-for-landlords-and-agents)). The notice periods are:

- **End of a fixed term:** at least 60 days, up from 28 ([CBS](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/sections/renting/renting/tenancy-reforms-for-landlords-and-agents))
- **Periodic tenancy, on a specified ground:** at least 90 days ([Law Handbook](https://lawhandbook.sa.gov.au/ch23s01s07s01.php))
- **Periodic tenancy, where you need the property back** to demolish, renovate, move in yourself or with immediate family, or hand over to a buyer: at least 60 days ([Law Handbook](https://lawhandbook.sa.gov.au/ch23s01s07s01.php))

**What to do:** Use the right CBS form, include the evidence CBS requires for your ground (see its [Evidence requirements for termination](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/documents/tenancy/fact-sheets/Evidence-requirements-for-termination.pdf) fact sheet), and remember that if you end a tenancy to renovate, sell or move in, you can't re-let the property as a home within 6 months unless SACAT agrees. SACAT can also declare a notice ineffective if it finds it was retaliatory ([CBS](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/sections/renting/renting/tenancy-reforms-for-landlords-and-agents)).

### Inspections capped at four a year

Routine inspections are limited to 4 a year, with 7 to 28 days' notice ([CBS rental reforms](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/campaigns/rental-reforms)). The notice must give a window of up to 2 hours, within 8am to 8pm on a day that isn't a Sunday or public holiday. Photos or video that capture the tenant's belongings need 7 days' notice and the tenant's written consent ([Law Handbook: Right of entry](https://lawhandbook.sa.gov.au/ch23s01s06s01.php)).

**What to do:** Plan inspections across the year, and check how you take photos.

### Pets

Tenants can ask to keep a pet using the CBS application form. You must reply in writing within 14 days, or approval is presumed. You can approve with reasonable conditions ([CBS summary](https://cbs.sa.gov.au/documents/tenancy/fact-sheets/Summary-of-tenancy-reforms-landlords-and-agents.pdf)), but you can only refuse on the grounds set out in the Act, and conditions can't include extra rent, a higher bond or a fee ([Law Handbook: Pets](https://lawhandbook.sa.gov.au/ch23s01s06s06.php)).

**What to do:** Diarise the 14 days and use CBS's response form. Our guide [can a landlord refuse pets in SA?](/blog/can-a-landlord-refuse-pets-sa/) covers the refusal grounds.

### Minimum housing standards, alterations and efficiency

- The property must meet minimum housing standards under the Housing Improvement Act 2016 on or before the day the tenant moves in. See our guide to [minimum housing standards](/blog/minimum-housing-standards-sa/).
- You can't unreasonably refuse minor alterations, or changes needed for disability, mobility or access. The tenant pays, and returns the property to how it was at the end.
- When you install or replace appliances, fittings or fixtures, they must meet the energy and water efficiency requirements in the regulations.

(All from the [CBS summary](https://cbs.sa.gov.au/documents/tenancy/fact-sheets/Summary-of-tenancy-reforms-landlords-and-agents.pdf).)

### Domestic abuse protections

A tenant experiencing domestic abuse can end the tenancy by notice with prescribed evidence, and a protected person with prescribed evidence can change the locks without your permission, as long as you're given a key ([CBS summary](https://cbs.sa.gov.au/documents/tenancy/fact-sheets/Summary-of-tenancy-reforms-landlords-and-agents.pdf)).

### Tenant information

You can't ask applicants for certain information, including tenancy dispute history, medical records, social media content, vehicle registration or why they're moving. From 1 September 2024, information from unsuccessful applicants must be destroyed 30 days after the property is let (or 6 months after they provided it, if they agree), and a successful tenant's information 3 years after the tenancy ends ([CBS rental reforms](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/campaigns/rental-reforms)).

**What to do:** Review what you collect, how it's stored, and when it's deleted.

## 5. A new ground for selling (1 September 2025)

**What changed:** Signing a sales agency agreement with a registered agent to sell the property is now a prescribed reason to end a tenancy. The new application form (see below) was also set in regulations from that date, and CBS updated its termination fact sheets ([CBS rental reforms](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/campaigns/rental-reforms)).

**What to do:** If you plan to sell, talk to your property manager before you sign with a sales agent. On this ground a periodic tenancy needs at least 60 days' notice, and a fixed-term tenancy can only be ended at the end of the term, also with at least 60 days' notice ([Law Handbook](https://lawhandbook.sa.gov.au/ch23s01s07s01.php)). The evidence rules apply, and so does the 6-month restriction on re-letting.

## 6. A standard application form and technical changes (January 2026)

**What changed:** From 1 January 2026, landlords and agents must use [Form A1](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/documents/tenancy/forms/Form-A1_Application-for-a-prospective-tenant-to-enter-into-a-residential-tenancy-1.pdf) to consider applicants. Each person who will be on the lease fills in their own form, and they can state a preferred lease length. Some landlords are exempt, including SA Housing Trust properties, NRAS participants, NDIS providers, registered community housing providers and registered charities ([CBS summary](https://cbs.sa.gov.au/documents/tenancy/fact-sheets/Summary-of-tenancy-reforms-landlords-and-agents.pdf)).

Then on 15 January 2026, smaller technical changes started ([CBS rental reforms](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/campaigns/rental-reforms)):

- Receipts can be issued electronically or on paper.
- The once-a-year limit rules out agreeing in advance to staged increases within a year.
- The 6-month wait before re-letting now runs from the date of termination, for both fixed-term and periodic tenancies.

**What to do:** Drop any old application forms. On Form A1 you fill in the address, lease length, rent and bond yourself.

## What's next

In September 2026 the government began consulting on a register of short-term rental properties ([CBS news](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/news/short-term-rental-register-to-help-address-rental-supply)).
