As at October 2026, a South Australian landlord can ask for a rental bond of up to 4 weeks' rent when the rent is $800 a week or less, or up to 6 weeks' rent when it's more than $800 a week. The bond isn't yours to hold: it has to be lodged with Consumer and Business Services (CBS) within 2 weeks if you manage the property yourself, or within 4 weeks if a registered agent manages it. It stays with CBS until the tenancy ends and a refund is agreed or decided.

Here's what SA landlords need to know about bonds, from the start of a lease to the end.

## How much bond can you charge?

The limit depends on the weekly rent. CBS's [bonds FAQs](https://cbs.sa.gov.au/sections/renting/bonds/bonds-faqs) set it out:

| Weekly rent | Maximum bond |
| ----------- | ------------ |
| $800 or less | 4 weeks' rent |
| More than $800 | 6 weeks' rent |

The $800 figure is set by the [Residential Tenancies Regulations 2025](https://www.legislation.sa.gov.au/__legislation/lz/c/r/residential%20tenancies%20regulations%202025/current/2025.91.auth.pdf) for section 61 of the [Residential Tenancies Act 1995](https://www.legislation.sa.gov.au/lz?path=%2Fc%2Fa%2Fresidential%20tenancies%20act%201995). Section 61 also says you can't require more than one bond for the same tenancy agreement, or a bond above the limit.

The 4-week cap for rents of $800 or less applies to new agreements that started after 1 April 2023, when CBS [lowered the bond for most rentals](https://cbs.sa.gov.au/news/more-affordable-rental-bonds). If your tenancy agreement was signed before then, an earlier threshold may apply to that bond. [CHECK: confirm with CBS how bonds under pre-1 April 2023 agreements are treated, including top-ups.]

When you receive bond money, the tenant must be given a receipt within 48 hours ([sa.gov.au: Rent and bond payments](https://www.sa.gov.au/topics/housing/renting-and-letting/renting-privately/start-of-tenancy/Rent-and-bond)).

## Who holds the bond?

CBS does. The bond is lodged with CBS in a trust account, and the landlord can't use it during the tenancy ([sa.gov.au: Rent and bond payments](https://www.sa.gov.au/topics/housing/renting-and-letting/renting-privately/start-of-tenancy/Rent-and-bond)).

### Lodgement deadlines

- **Self-managing landlords:** lodge within 2 weeks of receiving the bond.
- **Registered agents:** lodge within 4 weeks.

These timeframes are on the [sa.gov.au rent and bond page](https://www.sa.gov.au/topics/housing/renting-and-letting/renting-privately/start-of-tenancy/Rent-and-bond) and the CBS [bond lodgement form](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/documents/tenancy/forms/Bond-lodgement-form.pdf), which counts from when full or part payment is received.

### How to lodge

Bonds are lodged through Residential Bonds Online (RBO), CBS's online system. According to [sa.gov.au: Lodging a bond](https://www.sa.gov.au/topics/housing/renting-and-letting/residential-bonds/lodging-a-bond):

- Real estate agents must use RBO.
- Self-managing landlords can create their own RBO account to handle lodgement and refunds, or use the paper bond lodgement form by email or post.
- A paper form must be signed by both the landlord and the tenant.

Tenants can also lodge their bond directly with CBS, which then lets the landlord or agent know it has been received. [CHECK: confirm on a current CBS page that tenants can lodge directly, in force since 1 July 2024.]

## What happens when the agent or owner changes?

The bond stays with CBS. What changes is who is recorded as managing it.

In RBO, the new managing party sends a **bond import request** using the bond number, and the existing managing party approves it. The [RBO help page](https://cbs.sa.gov.au/sections/renting/bonds/residential-bonds-online-help-page) says the approving party has 14 days to act. If nobody does, the request is escalated to the CBS Bonds team. Once it's approved, the previous manager can no longer access the bond.

If RBO isn't being used, CBS has a [change of ownership/agent form](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/documents/tenancy/forms/Change_of_ownership_or_agent.pdf) for a change of management, a change of ownership, or both. A change of ownership needs a copy of ID and proof of ownership, such as a council or water rates notice. The previous and new parties both sign it, and it gives the date the change takes effect. CBS won't accept the form unless every section is complete.

If you're [switching property managers](/leasing/switching-property-managers/), ask the new agency to confirm the bond has been transferred to them, so nothing is left in limbo when the tenancy ends.

## Getting the bond back at the end of a tenancy

When the tenancy ends, the bond is refunded through CBS, not paid out by the landlord. Refunds are claimed in RBO ([sa.gov.au: Lodging a bond](https://www.sa.gov.au/topics/housing/renting-and-letting/residential-bonds/lodging-a-bond)) or with the CBS [bond refund form](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/documents/tenancy/forms/Bond_refund_form.pdf). If the landlord and tenant don't agree on who gets what, there's a set process.

According to CBS's [settling a bond dispute](https://cbs.sa.gov.au/sections/renting/bonds/settling-a-bond-dispute) page:

- If a tenant claims the bond without the landlord's agreement, the landlord or agent is notified and can dispute the claim. If they dispute it, they must lodge an online application with the South Australian Civil and Administrative Tribunal (SACAT). If they don't, the bond is paid to the tenant.
- If the landlord makes a claim and the tenant doesn't respond, the landlord or agent has to give CBS evidence for the claim. If the evidence isn't enough, the claim can be refused, and the landlord would then need to apply to SACAT.
- Through RBO, both sides can make counter-offers. Each has a 10-day timeframe, and there can be up to seven before the matter must go to SACAT. Once it goes to SACAT, CBS is no longer involved.

There's no deadline to claim a bond, and it stays with CBS until someone does. But if the property manager makes a claim 12 months or more after the tenancy ended, it goes straight to SACAT ([CBS bonds FAQs](https://cbs.sa.gov.au/sections/renting/bonds/bonds-faqs)).

SACAT says the most common end-of-tenancy dispute is a landlord claiming on the bond, or for compensation, because the property wasn't left in an appropriate condition ([SACAT: End of tenancy disputes](https://www.sacat.sa.gov.au/case-type/housing-and-rentals/end-of-tenancy-disputes)). Tenants can also apply to SACAT if their bond hasn't been refunded.

## The records that matter: inspection sheets

A disputed bond claim usually turns on evidence about the property's condition, starting with the inspection sheet (often called a condition report).

Under the [Residential Tenancies Regulations 2025](https://www.legislation.sa.gov.au/__legislation/lz/c/r/residential%20tenancies%20regulations%202025/current/2025.91.auth.pdf), when a tenant moves in, the landlord or agent must complete an inspection sheet and give each tenant one signed copy. The sheet must:

- identify the property
- list the fixtures, furniture and other contents in detail
- record the condition of the property and contents, as described by both landlord and tenant, at the start and end of the tenancy
- have space for both parties to sign at the start and the end
- tell the tenant they can contact CBS if there's a dispute about the condition.

CBS publishes an [inspection sheet](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/documents/tenancy/forms/Inspection_sheet.pdf) that meets these requirements, with condition codes and a water meter reading at the start and end.

If a claim reaches SACAT, it asks for documents including a statement of what's claimed and how much, the tenancy agreement, inspection sheets and photos, invoices or quotes, rent records and any notices served ([SACAT: Providing documents for housing disputes](https://www.sacat.sa.gov.au/case-type/housing-and-rentals/providing-documents-for-housing-disputes)).

In practice, a detailed entry inspection with dated photos, a matching exit inspection, and quotes or invoices for anything you claim make a bond much easier to settle fairly. Getting the property in good order first also gives you a clear baseline: see [getting your property ready to rent](/blog/how-to-prepare-your-property-for-rent/).
